Plain-language glossary

Lending language, decoded.

A shared vocabulary for customers, product teams, technology teams and partners.

APR
An annualised expression of the cost of credit. It helps compare offers when calculated and disclosed consistently.
Beneficial owner
The person who ultimately owns, controls or benefits from a business, even if another legal entity is shown on paper.
Cooling-off period
A disclosed period in which an eligible borrower may exit a digital loan under the applicable terms and charges.
DLA
Digital Lending App: the customer-facing application or web surface through which a lending journey is delivered.
HFC
Housing Finance Company: a regulated entity focused primarily on housing finance and related products.
KFS
Key Facts Statement: the important cost, term, charge, cooling-off and grievance information provided before execution.
LMS
Loan Management System: the account, schedule, ledger, payment, servicing, collection and closure layer after sanction/disbursement.
LOS
Loan Origination System: the application, verification, underwriting, offer, KFS, sanction and disbursement-readiness layer.
LSP
Lending Service Provider: a partner performing contracted lending-related functions on behalf of a regulated entity.
LWS
Loan Workflow System: the human work, maker-checker, exception, complaint, partner and operational orchestration layer.
Maker-checker
A control pattern where one person prepares an action and another appropriately authorised person reviews or approves it.
NBFC
Non-Banking Financial Company: a company authorised to conduct specified financial activities under its applicable regulatory category.
Regulated entity (RE)
The bank or other regulated financial institution accountable for the lending product and customer outcome.
Tenant
In LoanOS, the isolated platform boundary for one contracting regulated entity and its authorised users, partners and records.

Common questions

The short answers.

Is LoanOS a lender or loan marketplace?

No. LoanOS is lending technology for regulated institutions and their authorised partners. It does not itself offer loans, promise approval or set a customer’s rate.

Which organisations can use LoanOS?

The target ecosystem includes commercial and small finance banks, co-operative banks, NBFCs, HFCs, AIFIs, and controlled participation by LSPs, DLAs, fintechs, DSAs, co-lenders and specialist service providers.

Which loan types are supported?

LoanOS provides configurable patterns across business, retail, secured, asset, trade-linked and specialised journeys. Actual availability and production readiness depend on institutional product policy, integrations, configuration and review.

Can an institution start with one module?

Yes. The recommended approach is to start with one meaningful end-to-end journey and enable the product planes required for it, while preserving the common customer and evidence spine.

Does LoanOS replace core banking?

Not necessarily. LoanOS can act as the lending operating layer around existing systems of record and specialist services. The target architecture depends on the institution’s current estate and product model.

Is the demonstration environment production-ready?

No. It demonstrates working product and control foundations. Production use requires deployment-specific security, resilience, integration, operational, legal and compliance readiness work.
